Foundation 1927

Genesis of the Soldiers for Equality

Born out of the historic demand for civil rights, self-respect, and the eradication of caste oppression.

Why Babasaheb Formed Samata Sainik Dal

In the 1920s, as Dr. B.R. Ambedkar spearheaded the mass awakening of the depressed and marginalized classes in India, orthodox reactionary forces constantly sought to disrupt public meetings, water access struggles, and social reform assemblies.

Recognizing that a non-violent social movement requires ironclad discipline, physical defense, and organized youth volunteerism, Babasaheb formally instituted the Samata Sainik Dal (समता सैनिक दल) on 24 September 1927 at Damodar Hall, Parel, Bombay, during the preparatory phases of the historic Mahad Satyagraha.

The Core Motto of SSD

"Liberty, Equality, Fraternity" (समता, स्वतंत्रता, बंधुता) and "Educate, Agitate, Organize" (शिक्षित बनो, संघर्ष करो, संगठित रहो). Every Sainik is sworn to protect democratic freedom, constitutional values, and community self-respect.

Epoch-Making Struggles

Historic Milestones of the Movement

Moments where Samata Sainik Dal stood as an unyielding wall of peaceful defense for human dignity.

1. Mahad Chavdar Tale Satyagraha & Manusmriti Dahan (1927)

During the historic Mahad movement on 19–20 March 1927 and 25 December 1927, thousands of delegates assembled to assert the right to drink water from the public Chavdar Lake. Over 1,000 disciplined SSD volunteers guarded the Satyagraha camp day and night, ensured zero violence, and protected Dr. Ambedkar during the historic burning of the Manusmriti on 25 December 1927 (celebrated annually as Bharatiya Stree Mukti Din / Social Equality Day).

2. Kalaram Mandir Entry Movement, Nashik (1930–1935)

Under the direct guidance of Babasaheb and Bhaurao Gaikwad, hundreds of SSD cadets in crisp white uniforms marched in military precision to assert civil and temple entry rights at the Kalaram Temple in Nashik, demonstrating unprecedented non-violent endurance in the face of brutal police lathicharges and conservative resistance.

3. Historic Deekshabhoomi Dhamma Deeksha Parade (14 October 1956)

When Bodhisattva Dr. B.R. Ambedkar embraced Buddhism along with over 500,000 followers at Nagpur, Samata Sainik Dal took full charge of the ceremonial parade, guard of honor, crowd mobilization, and public safety across the sprawling Deekshabhoomi grounds. The magnificent uniform march-past remains one of the proudest chapters in Ambedkarite history.

4. Centenary of SSD (1927–2027)

As Samata Sainik Dal approaches its 100th Foundation Anniversary in 2027, the National Central Command is leading nationwide constitutional awareness yatras, modernizing youth cadet drills, establishing 100 Dr. Ambedkar study libraries, and training 100,000 new Sainiks for national service.

Constitution & Bylaws

Organizational Structure & Sainik Code

Governed by the democratic principles of the Constitution of India and Dr. Ambedkar's directives.

Strict Secular & Democratic Ethos

Open to every citizen committed to social equality, anti-caste fraternity, and constitutional morality regardless of caste, gender, or religion.

The Official Uniform

White/Khaki uniform, blue cap/beret, black belt, and the Dhamma Wheel brass insignia representing discipline, vigilance, and self-respect.

Democratic Leadership

Central Command, State Commanders, District Nayaks, and Unit Captains democratically coordinated for community welfare and legal aid.
